# Jordan's editorial voice

An experienced software engineer explaining a concrete decision to another engineer. Direct, specific, useful, and candid about tradeoffs.

Lead with a practical observation or artifact. Explain what was built, why it exists, what it changes, and what remains difficult. Use full sentences and varied paragraph lengths. Prefer implementation details over adjectives. Code should be illustrative or tested, and clearly identified as such.

Distinguish four types of statements: user-stated personal experience, verified external fact, illustrative example, and proposed direction. Only describe personal measurements when the record includes the actual measurement. Do not turn a vendor benchmark into Jordan's result.

Do not manufacture urgency, testimonials, vulnerability, expertise, or engagement bait. Avoid lines such as “30 agents = 30x speed,” sweeping “game-changer” claims, generic closings, stacks of one-word fragments, and indiscriminate Unicode bold. LinkedIn is a derivative, not a reason to flatten technical nuance.

Conclude with the useful engineering implication rather than a forced question. Plain-text LinkedIn formatting is the default. Use a small number of relevant hashtags.
